05 — RISK × VOLUME

Online travel agents account for more cancellations than Groups

In the combined view, Online TA has a lower cancellation rate than Groups, but almost three times as many bookings. Multiplying each group’s count by its reported rate gives approximately 20,727 cancellations for Online TA, versus 12,105 for Groups. The two segments represent about 74% of estimated cancellations among displayed segments.

Cancellation priorities
Market segmentBookingsCancellation rateEstimated cancellationsShare of displayed cancellations

Estimated counts = bookings × rounded cancellation rate. Rounding is not sampling uncertainty: a rate rounded to 0.1 percentage point implies a possible count difference of about ±0.0005 × bookings. For Online TA in the combined view, that is about ±28 records. The displayed segments omit two records; shares use only the displayed segments.

Practical implication: a modest improvement in a large segment may prevent more cancellations than a large improvement in a small one. Actual priorities also depend on whether cancellations can be prevented, the cost of doing so, and whether rooms would otherwise be resold.

Market segment, distribution channel and booking window are overlapping descriptions of the same bookings. Their estimated cancellation counts must not be added together, and the summaries cannot identify the intersection “Online TA bookings over 100 days.”

Booking-window mix alone does not account for the difference. City Hotel’s reported cancellation rate is higher within every band. The common-mix comparison leaves an 11.24-percentage-point gap; customer composition, contract terms, prices and dates may still differ.

How the calculation works

Use the combined dataset’s booking share in each lead-time band as a common weight. Multiply each property’s band-specific cancellation rate by these weights and sum. The observed rate uses the property’s own weights. This adjusts one dimension only, using rounded aggregate rates. It is not a causal effect of hotel type or a prediction for changing the booking mix. The source label “100+” is interpreted as 101+ because the preceding band includes day 100 and the source sort key is 101; the original SQL is needed to verify that boundary.

07 — CORRECTING THE CALENDAR COMPARISON

August’s total includes an extra year of observations

The page states a July 2015–August 2017 window. If that coverage is complete, July and August appear three times and every other month twice. Comparing their raw counts exaggerates their relative volume.

In the combined monthly series, August has 8,638 records versus May’s 7,114. Dividing by month occurrences gives approximately 2,879 per August and 3,557 per May. May becomes the highest-volume month on this limited normalization. It does not become the month with proven highest demand or occupancy.

The population also needs reconciliation. Monthly counts sum to 75,166, not 119,389. That is close to the non-canceled population implied by the headline cancellation rate, suggesting—but not confirming—a non-canceled-booking filter. Until the SQL is recovered, call these published monthly counts, not total demand.

For a stronger seasonal analysis, compare the same calendar period across years, account for days per month and available rooms, separate cancellations from arrivals, and distinguish booking-weighted rates from room-night-weighted ADR. A booking count does not measure occupancy; room inventory and nights are missing here.

The final column divides the source’s revenue measure by all listed bookings in that channel, including canceled bookings in the denominator. It is not room rate, customer lifetime value or profit. The revenue numerator’s treatment of nights, deposits and refunds cannot be checked without the original query.

Compare net contribution after agent commissions, direct-marketing costs, service costs and refunds before shifting budget. Direct bookings may be different customers choosing different stays. Lower historical cancellations do not show that moving an otherwise identical booking to Direct would change its behavior.

09 — WHEN DOES AN INTERVENTION PAY?

Preventing a cancellation matters only if it adds net value

A canceled room may be resold, sometimes at a better rate. Retained deposits may offset part of the loss. Therefore, estimated cancellations multiplied by ADR is not a defensible revenue-loss estimate.

Illustrative break-even calculator

Use the selected hotel’s segment volume as a historical reference cohort. Specify an absolute reduction in cancellation probability, incremental value per prevented cancellation, and cost per booking contacted. These are assumptions—not estimated treatment effects or a forecast.

Expected net value = bookings × [(reduction in percentage points ÷ 100) × incremental value − contact cost]. The value assumption must reflect room resale, variable service costs and changes in deposit or cancellation-fee income; contact cost should include allocated setup costs. The scenario assumes every booking in the selected segment is contacted. Fractional expected counts are possible. A 2-point reduction means 36.7% becomes 34.7%, not a 2% relative reduction.

Risk is not responsiveness: the bookings most likely to cancel may be least affected by a reminder or flexible rebooking offer. Choose interventions based on measured incremental value, not cancellation probability alone.

A focused pilot

With future booking records, randomly assign eligible bookings to the current process or a reminder/reconfirmation offer, respecting group-booking clusters. Define eligibility, contact timing, a meaningful minimum effect and test duration in advance. Measure net contribution over completed stay outcomes, including resale, refunds and contact costs. Track cancellation timing, conversion to stays, guest complaints and repeat bookings as secondary outcomes. A reminder that brings cancellations forward may help resell rooms even if it does not lower the final cancellation rate.

If a prediction model is developed

Use only fields available at the moment a decision is made. Exclude final reservation status and final-status date, and verify timestamps for deposits, changes and room assignments. Train on earlier periods and test on later ones, with repeated guests or group reservations handled to avoid leakage. Assess calibration and economic value, rather than accuracy alone. The published summaries cannot support training or validating such a model.

Conclusion for management: prioritize the combination of cancellation volume, preventability and incremental value. The summaries point toward Online TA, Groups and long booking windows for investigation, while the property comparison shows that lead-time mix is only part of the city–resort difference. Test a specific intervention before changing channel strategy, deposit policy or overbooking levels.
